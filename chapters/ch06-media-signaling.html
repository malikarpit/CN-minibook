6.1 Classification: Guided vs. Unguided Media

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Transmission Media — Guided vs Unguided Classification
Transmission Medium — Definition & Taxonomy
A transmission medium is the physical conduit situated beneath the OSI Physical Layer through which electromagnetic or optical energy signals propagate from sender to receiver. Transmission media are broadly classified into two categories:
  • Guided (Bounded / Wired) Media: The signal waves are physically constrained and directed along a solid physical path (copper wire, glass strand).
  • Unguided (Unbounded / Wireless) Media: Electromagnetic waves propagate freely through the atmosphere, vacuum, or water without physical conductors.

6.2 Twisted Pair Cable (UTP & STP)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Guided Media — Twisted Pair (Physics, Categories & RJ-45)
Twisted Pair Cable — First-Principles Definition
A twisted pair cable consists of two individually insulated copper wires twisted together in a helical spiral pattern. One wire carries the signal, while the other serves as the ground/differential reference. The continuous twisting cancels electromagnetic interference (EMI) and eliminates crosstalk from adjacent pairs.

Why It Exists: Overcoming Parallel Wire Crosstalk

If two copper wires run parallel to each other, external noise sources (power lines, electric motors, radio transmitters) induce significantly different voltages on the wire closer to the noise source than on the wire further away. This differential voltage corrupts the signal. By twisting the wires helically:

  • Each wire alternates between being closer to and further from the noise source.
  • On average over the length of the cable, both conductors absorb an identical amount of noise voltage ($V_{\text{noise}}$).

The Mathematical Proof of Differential Noise Cancellation

Modern Ethernet utilizes balanced differential signaling:

  1. The transmitter sends $+V_s / 2$ on Conductor A and $-V_s / 2$ on Conductor B (total signal difference $= V_s$).
  2. External noise induces identical noise voltage $V_{\text{noise}}$ on both tightly twisted conductors: $$V_A = +V_s / 2 + V_{\text{noise}}$$ $$V_B = -V_s / 2 + V_{\text{noise}}$$
  3. The receiver uses a differential operational amplifier that measures the difference between the two conductors: $$V_{\text{received}} = V_A - V_B = \left(+V_s / 2 + V_{\text{noise}}\right) - \left(-V_s / 2 + V_{\text{noise}}\right) = V_s$$
  4. Result: $V_{\text{noise}} - V_{\text{noise}} = 0$. The external noise is eliminated mathematically!

6.3 Coaxial Cable (Coax)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Guided Media — Coaxial Cable (Structure, Standards & Connectors)
Coaxial Cable — Definition & Anatomy
A coaxial cable carries signals of higher frequency ranges than twisted pair. It consists of a central solid or stranded copper core conductor surrounded by four concentric layers:
  1. Inner Core: Solid copper wire carrying the signal.
  2. Dielectric Insulator: Non-conductive flexible plastic layer keeping concentric spacing constant.
  3. Outer Conductor / Shield: Braided metallic mesh providing ground return and shielding from outside RF noise.
  4. Outer Jacket: Tough plastic/PVC sheath protecting against physical damage and moisture.

6.4 Optical Fiber Cable (Physics & TIR)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Guided Media — Optical Fiber (Snell's Law, TIR, SMF vs MMF)
Optical Fiber — First-Principles Definition
An optical fiber is a dielectric cylindrical waveguide made of ultra-pure silica glass ($SiO_2$) or plastic that transmits data encoded as pulses of light via the physical phenomenon of Total Internal Reflection (TIR).

The Physics of Total Internal Reflection

A fiber consists of an inner core with refractive index $n_1$ surrounded by a concentric cladding with a lower refractive index $n_2$ ($n_1 > n_2$).

  • Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2$.
  • Critical Angle ($\theta_c$): When light moves from a denser medium ($n_1$) to a rarer medium ($n_2$), as the angle of incidence increases, the refracted angle reaches $90^\circ$. Setting $\theta_2 = 90^\circ$: $$\sin \theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1} \implies \theta_c = \arcsin\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)$$
  • TIR Condition: If the angle of incidence at the core-cladding boundary exceeds the critical angle ($\theta > \theta_c$), zero light is refracted into the cladding; 100% of the photon energy is reflected back into the core, guiding the light along the fiber.
  • Numerical Aperture ($NA$): The light-gathering capacity of the fiber from air ($n_0 = 1$): $$NA = \sqrt{n_1^2 - n_2^2} = \sin \theta_{\text{acceptance}}$$

6.5 Unguided Media: Radio Waves (3 kHz to 1 GHz)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Unguided Media — Radio Waves (Characteristics & Propagation)
Radio Waves — Operational Properties
Electromagnetic waves ranging from 3 kHz to 1 GHz are called radio waves. Radio waves are omnidirectional: when an antenna transmits, electromagnetic energy radiates in all directions, allowing receiving antennas to capture signals without precise physical alignment.

Propagation Modes Across the Radio Spectrum

  • Ground Wave Propagation (< 2 MHz): Waves follow the physical curvature of the Earth through the troposphere (AM radio, VLF submarine communications).
  • Sky Wave Propagation (2 MHz to 30 MHz): Higher frequency radio waves radiate upward and refract off the ionized gas layers of the upper atmosphere (ionosphere), bouncing back to Earth. Enables intercontinental shortwave radio broadcasts.
  • Line-of-Sight Space Wave (> 30 MHz): Waves travel directly in a straight line from transmitting antenna to receiving antenna without atmospheric reflection (VHF/UHF television, FM radio, cellular networks).

6.6 Unguided Media: Microwaves (1 GHz to 300 GHz)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Unguided Media — Terrestrial Microwaves (Line-of-Sight & Antennas)
Microwaves — Definition & Properties
Electromagnetic waves ranging from 1 GHz to 300 GHz are called microwaves. Unlike omnidirectional radio waves, microwaves are unidirectional: transmitting and receiving antennas must be narrowly focused and aligned precisely along a direct optical line of sight.

Physical Constraints of Terrestrial Microwave Links

  • Parabolic Dish Antennas: Geometry focuses incoming parallel rays into a single focal point, amplifying signal gain.
  • Earth Curvature Horizon Limit: Because microwaves travel in straight lines, the maximum distance $d$ between two line-of-sight towers of heights $h_1$ and $h_2$ is governed by Earth's curvature: $$d \approx 3.57 \left(\sqrt{h_1} + \sqrt{h_2}\right)\ \text{km}$$
  • Rain Fade: At frequencies above 10 GHz, atmospheric water droplets absorb and scatter microwave energy, causing attenuation during heavy rainfall.

6.7 Unguided Media: Infrared (300 GHz to 400 THz)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Unguided Media — Infrared Transmission & Confinement
Infrared — Characteristics
Infrared signals span frequencies from 300 GHz to 400 THz (wavelengths from $1\ \text{mm}$ to $770\ \text{nm}$). Used for short-range communication in closed rooms (television remote controls, wireless keyboards, legacy IrDA).

The Wall Confinement Principle & Security Advantage

Unlike radio waves and microwaves, infrared radiation cannot penetrate solid walls:

  • Interference Immunity: An infrared communication system in Room A cannot interfere with another infrared system operating on the same frequency in adjacent Room B.
  • Physical Security: Eavesdropping from outside the building is physically impossible without direct line-of-sight access through a window.
  • Limitation: Useless for outdoor long-distance transmission because sunlight contains immense infrared energy that blinds receivers.

6.8 Satellite Communication (GEO, MEO, LEO)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Unguided Media — Satellite Communication (Orbits & Latency)
Satellite Transponder — Definition
A communication satellite functions as an orbital microwave relay station. It receives an uplink transmission from a ground station on one frequency band (e.g., $6\ \text{GHz}$), regenerates/amplifies the signal, and retransmits it on a different downlink frequency band (e.g., $4\ \text{GHz}$) to avoid self-interference.

6.10 Line Coding Fundamentals

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Signaling: Digital-to-Digital Conversion (Line Coding Principles)
Line Coding — Definition & Purpose
Line coding is the process of converting discrete digital data (sequences of bits: $0\text{s}$ and $1\text{s}$) into discrete digital signals (time-varying voltage or current pulses) for transmission over a guided transmission link.

The 4 Desirable Characteristics of Line Codes

  1. Zero DC Component: If a signal has a constant non-zero average voltage, it accumulates a Direct Current (DC) component. DC energy cannot pass through electrical transformers or capacitive isolation blocks used in telephone lines and Ethernet ports, generating excess heat and signal distortion. An ideal line code has a zero mean voltage.
  2. Self-Clocking / Synchronization: Transmitter and receiver have separate internal crystal clocks. Over time, clock drift occurs. A line code must provide frequent voltage transitions so the receiver can lock its phase-locked loop (PLL) clock directly to the incoming data stream without a separate physical clock wire.
  3. Immunity to Baseline Wander: The receiver evaluates incoming voltages relative to an average running threshold ("baseline"). Long runs of $1\text{s}$ or $0\text{s}$ cause the baseline to drift, resulting in decoding bit errors.
  4. Bandwidth Efficiency: Minimizing the required signaling rate (baud rate) to maximize data throughput within a limited channel bandwidth.

6.11 Non-Return-to-Zero (NRZ-L & NRZ-I)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Line Coding Schemes — NRZ-L and NRZ-I Waveforms
Non-Return-to-Zero (NRZ) Schemes
In NRZ encoding, the electrical signal maintains a constant voltage level throughout the entire duration of a bit interval; it never "returns to zero" volts in the middle of a bit.
  • NRZ-L (NRZ-Level): The voltage level depends strictly on the state of the bit. Typically: Positive Voltage $= 0$, Negative Voltage $= 1$.
  • NRZ-I (NRZ-Invert): The signal level inverts (transitions) at the boundary of a bit interval if the bit is a $1$; no transition occurs if the bit is a $0$.

Strengths & Fatal Flaws

  • Strengths: Highly bandwidth efficient ($S = N/2$). Simple transmitter hardware.
  • Fatal Flaws: Heavy DC component. In NRZ-L, long runs of $0\text{s}$ or $1\text{s}$ produce flat DC lines with zero transitions, causing receiver clock desynchronization. In NRZ-I, long runs of $0\text{s}$ produce no transitions, causing identical clock failure.

6.12 Manchester Encoding (IEEE 802.3 Standard)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Line Coding Schemes — Manchester Encoding & Timing Diagrams
Manchester Encoding — First-Principles Definition
Manchester encoding is a biphase line coding technique where an electrical transition always occurs exactly at the midpoint of every bit interval. The mid-bit transition provides both clock synchronization and data representation.
  • IEEE 802.3 Standard Convention:
    • Bit $0$ = High-to-Low transition at midpoint.
    • Bit $1$ = Low-to-High transition at midpoint.
  • Thomas / G.E. Thomas Convention: Exactly the opposite (Low-to-High for 0, High-to-Low for 1). University exams follow IEEE 802.3 unless explicitly stated.
Manchester Waveform Timing Diagram (IEEE 802.3)

   Bit Sequence:         0             1             0             1
   Mid-bit Point:        ▲             ▲             ▲             ▲
                         │             │             │             │
   Waveform:       ┌─────┐             ┌─────┐ ┌─────┐             ┌─────┐
   (IEEE 802.3)    │     │             │     │ │     │             │     │
                   ┘     └─────────────┘     └─┘     └─────────────┘     └───
                   [High -> Low] [Low -> High] [High -> Low] [Low -> High]
                     (Bit 0)       (Bit 1)       (Bit 0)       (Bit 1)

The Inherent Bandwidth Trade-Off

  • Advantages: 100% self-clocking (transition guaranteed every single bit). Zero DC component (voltage is high for 50% and low for 50% of every bit interval).
  • Disadvantage: Because each bit requires two signal levels, the signaling rate is double the bit rate ($S = 2N$). A 10 Mbps Ethernet system requires a 20 MHz baud rate.

6.13 Differential Manchester Encoding (Token Ring)

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Line Coding Schemes — Differential Manchester (IEEE 802.5)
Differential Manchester — Rules of Construction
Standardized in IEEE 802.5 Token Ring, Differential Manchester uses the presence or absence of a transition at the beginning of the bit interval to represent data:
  1. A transition always occurs at the midpoint of every bit interval (solely for clock synchronization).
  2. Bit 0: A transition occurs at the beginning of the bit interval.
  3. Bit 1: NO transition occurs at the beginning of the bit interval.
Differential Manchester Waveform

   Bit:              0             1             0             0
   Start Transition: YES           NO            YES           YES
   Mid Transition:   ALWAYS        ALWAYS        ALWAYS        ALWAYS

   Waveform:       ┌─────┐       ┌───────────┐   ┌─────┐       ┌─────┐
                   │     │       │           │   │     │       │     │
               ────┘     └───────┘           └───┘     └───────┘     └───
💡
Why "Differential" Matters: Wire Inversion Immunity In standard Manchester, if an installer accidentally swaps the two wires in an RJ-45 plug, every $0$ becomes a $1$ and every $1$ becomes a $0$! Differential Manchester depends only on the presence/absence of transitions, making it completely immune to 180° wire polarity inversion.

6.15 Summary & High-Yield Exam Traps

🎓 Uni Required Prescribed syllabus topic: Chapter 6 Revision Checklist & Common Pitfalls

Five Critical University Exam Traps

Trap ID Common Misconception First-Principles Reality & Refutation
Trap 1 "Manchester encoding is more bandwidth-efficient than NRZ." False! Manchester requires double the bandwidth of NRZ because it requires two signal transitions per bit interval ($r = 1/2$).
Trap 2 "Optical fiber cladding has a higher refractive index than the core." Fatal Error! For Total Internal Reflection (TIR) to occur, the core MUST be optically denser than the cladding ($n_{\text{core}} > n_{\text{cladding}}$).
Trap 3 "Single-mode fiber is called 'single' because it transmits in only one direction." False! "Single-mode" refers to light propagating as a single electromagnetic ray/mode along the axial core, eliminating modal dispersion.
Trap 4 "Differential Manchester determines bit values by the midpoint transition." False! The midpoint transition is solely for clock recovery. Bit values are determined by the presence or absence of a transition at the beginning of the bit interval!
Trap 5 "LEO satellites experience higher latency than GEO satellites." False! LEO satellites orbit at 500–1200 km (latency $\sim 20 - 40\ \text{ms}$), while GEO satellites orbit at 35,786 km (latency $\sim 270\ \text{ms}$).
