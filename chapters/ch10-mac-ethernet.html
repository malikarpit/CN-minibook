Section 10.1

The MAC Sublayer & Channel Allocation Problem 🎓 Uni Unit II: MAC Protocols

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (The MAC Sublayer & Channel Allocation Problem)

Network links are divided into two fundamental topologies: Point-to-Point links (dedicated connection between exactly two endpoints, e.g., PPP) and Broadcast / Multiaccess links (multiple nodes share a single common transmission medium, e.g., Ethernet bus, Wi-Fi radio airwaves).

The Medium Access Control (MAC) Sublayer

When two or more machines transmit signals simultaneously onto a shared multiaccess broadcast medium, their electrical or radio signals physically collide and garble one another, resulting in total data loss. The lower half of OSI Layer 2—the Medium Access Control (MAC) sublayer—provides protocols to arbitrate access and determine who transmits next on the shared channel.

Section 10.2

Pure ALOHA Protocol (Maximum Throughput: 18.4%) 🎓 Uni Unit II: ALOHA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Pure ALOHA Protocol & 18.4% Throughput Analysis)

Invented in 1970 by Norman Abramson at the University of Hawaii to connect island campuses via ground-based radio, Pure ALOHA is the grandfather of all random access protocols.

Pure ALOHA Transmission Rule

Whenever a station has a frame to transmit, it transmits immediately! Stations never sense the channel before sending. If a collision occurs, the sender waits a random backoff time and retransmits.

The Vulnerable Time of Pure ALOHA

Let $T_{\text{fr}}$ be the frame transmission time. If Station A begins transmitting at time $t_0$, any other station that begins transmitting between $t_0 - T_{\text{fr}}$ and $t_0 + T_{\text{fr}}$ will collide with Station A!

Pure ALOHA Vulnerable Period & Throughput (GATE Formula)
\text{Vulnerable Period } = 2 \times T_{\text{fr}}
S = G \cdot e^{-2G}
• $G$ = Traffic offered load (average number of frames generated per frame time $T_{\text{fr}}$)
• $S$ = Throughput (rate of successfully delivered frames)
• Maximum throughput occurs at $G = 0.5$:
$$S_{\max} = 0.5 \cdot e^{-1} = \frac{1}{2e} \approx \mathbf{0.184} = \mathbf{18.4\%}$$
Section 10.3

Slotted ALOHA (Maximum Throughput: 36.8%)

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Slotted ALOHA Protocol & 36.8% Throughput Analysis)

In 1972, Roberts doubled the capacity of ALOHA by introducing a shared clock that divides time into discrete synchronized time slots of length $T_{\text{fr}}$.

Slotted ALOHA Rule

A station is only allowed to transmit at the beginning of a time slot. If a frame arrives in the middle of a slot, it must wait until the start of the next slot. This halves the collision window!

Slotted ALOHA Vulnerable Period & Throughput
\text{Vulnerable Period } = 1 \times T_{\text{fr}}
S = G \cdot e^{-G}
• Maximum throughput occurs at $G = 1.0$:
$$S_{\max} = 1.0 \cdot e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e} \approx \mathbf{0.368} = \mathbf{36.8\%}$$ 👉 Slotted ALOHA exactly DOUBLES the throughput of Pure ALOHA!
Section 10.4

Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA) Fundamentals 🎓 Uni Unit II: CSMA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Carrier Sense Multiple Access — CSMA Fundamentals)

ALOHA is fundamentally wasteful because stations broadcast blindly without checking if someone else is already talking. CSMA introduces a civilized rule: "Listen before you speak" (Carrier Sensing).

⚠️ Why Do Collisions Still Occur in CSMA? (Propagation Delay)

If Station A senses the channel as idle and starts transmitting, its electrical signal takes time $T_p$ to travel down the wire to Station B. If Station B senses the channel during that brief propagation window, the wire still appears idle! Station B begins transmitting, and the two wavefronts collide in the middle of the cable!

Section 10.5

1-Persistent CSMA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (1-Persistent CSMA)
1-Persistent CSMA Rules

1. Sense the channel.
2. If channel is IDLE: Transmit immediately with probability 1.0.
3. If channel is BUSY: Continuously monitor and listen to the wire. The very instant the channel becomes idle, transmit immediately!
• Flaw: If two or more stations were both patiently listening while a third station was talking, both will pounce on the wire the instant it goes quiet $\implies$ Guaranteed collision!

Section 10.6

Non-Persistent CSMA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Non-Persistent CSMA)
Non-Persistent CSMA Rules

1. Sense the channel.
2. If channel is IDLE: Transmit immediately.
3. If channel is BUSY: Do NOT continuously monitor the line! Wait a random amount of time and then sense the channel again.
• Benefit: Dramatically reduces collisions because waiting stations wake up at different random times.
• Drawback: Increases idle channel delay; the line may sit idle while stations are asleep.

Section 10.7

p-Persistent CSMA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (p-Persistent CSMA)
p-Persistent CSMA (Used in Slotted Channels)

When the channel becomes idle, the station transmits with probability $p$, and defers transmission to the next time slot with probability $(1 - p)$. Strikes an optimal balance between collision rate and idle latency.

Section 10.8

CSMA/CD (Collision Detection) & Minimum Frame Size (IEEE 802.3) 🎓 Uni Unit II: CSMA/CD

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (CSMA/CD — Collision Detection & IEEE 802.3 Ethernet)

In standard CSMA, when a collision occurs, both stations blindly finish transmitting their entire multi-kilobyte frames, wasting channel time. CSMA/CD introduces: "Listen while you speak" (Collision Detection).

CSMA/CD Protocol Steps

1. Listen Before Talk: Sense carrier. Wait until idle.
2. Listen While Talk: Monitor the voltage on the wire while transmitting. If the observed voltage exceeds normal levels, a Collision is Detected!
3. Abort & Jam: Instantly stop frame transmission and blast a 32-bit Jam Signal so all other nodes detect the collision.
4. Binary Exponential Backoff: After collision number $i$, pick a random integer $k$ from range $[0, 2^i - 1]$ (capped at $i = 10$). Wait $k \times \text{Slot Time}$ before attempting to retransmit. Discard frame after 16 failed attempts.

The Fundamental Minimum Frame Size Theorem (High-Yield GATE Proof)

For a station to detect a collision, it must still be actively transmitting when the reflected collision signal returns to it! If a frame is too small, transmission finishes before the collision wavefront returns, and the sender mistakenly assumes the frame was delivered safely!

CSMA/CD Minimum Frame Size Formula
T_{\text{fr}} \ge 2 \times T_p
\frac{L_{\min}}{B} \ge 2 \times \frac{d}{v} \implies L_{\min} = 2 \times T_p \times B
• $L_{\min}$ = Minimum frame size in bits
• $B$ = Channel bandwidth (bps)
• $T_p$ = Maximum propagation delay between the two farthest nodes ($d/v$)
• In standard 10 Mbps Ethernet over 2.5 km coaxial: $2T_p \approx 51.2\ \mu\text{s} \implies L_{\min} = 51.2\ \mu\text{s} \times 10\text{ Mbps} = \mathbf{512\text{ bits}} = \mathbf{64\text{ bytes}}$!
Section 10.9

CSMA/CA (Collision Avoidance) & RTS/CTS (IEEE 802.11 Wi-Fi) 🎓 Uni Unit II: CSMA/CA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (CSMA/CA — Collision Avoidance & IEEE 802.11 Wi-Fi)
⚠️ Why CSMA/CD Is Impossible in Wireless Wi-Fi!

In wireless communication, a transmitting antenna radiates high power locally, drowning out faint incoming radio signals by a factor of 100,000 to 1 ($50\text{ dB}$ difference). A station cannot hear incoming collisions while it is shouting into the air! Therefore, Wi-Fi must use Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA).

The Four Pillars of CSMA/CA

  • Inter-Frame Space (IFS): When the channel is idle, stations must pause for an IFS duration (SIFS < PIFS < DIFS) before transmitting. Highest priority traffic (ACKs) uses the shortest gap (SIFS).
  • Contention Window Backoff: Stations select a random backoff countdown even if the channel is idle.
  • RTS / CTS Handshake: Sender transmits a short Request to Send (RTS) frame. Receiver replies with a Clear to Send (CTS) broadcast frame before bulk data is sent.
  • Network Allocation Vector (NAV): Every frame carries a duration field. All listening nodes set a software timer (NAV) and go to sleep for that duration, avoiding collisions!
Section 10.10

The Hidden and Exposed Terminal Problems

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Hidden & Exposed Terminal Problems in Wireless MAC)
Figure 10.2: The Hidden Terminal Problem
       [ Node A ] --------> [ Node B (AP) ] <-------- [ Node C ]
     Radio Range of A:       In Range of A & C       Radio Range of C:
      Covers B, NOT C                                 Covers B, NOT A

   • Node A wants to send to B. Senses medium -> Sees idle (can't hear C!).
   • Node C wants to send to B. Senses medium -> Sees idle (can't hear A!).
   • BOTH transmit simultaneously! -> COLLISION AT RECEIVER B! 💥
   👉 Solved by RTS/CTS: Node B broadcasts CTS, which Node C hears and backs off!
Figure 10.3: The Exposed Terminal Problem
       [ Node A ] <======== [ Node B ]    [ Node C ] ========> [ Node D ]
                             (B sends to A) (C wants to send to D)

   • Node C hears B transmitting to A. C falsely assumes the medium is busy.
   • C defers transmission to D unnecessarily, even though D is far away from B!
   👉 Wastes wireless capacity. Solved by directional antennas or RTS/CTS.
Section 10.11

Master Comparison: CSMA/CD vs CSMA/CA

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Master Comparison: CSMA/CD vs CSMA/CA)
Feature CSMA/CD (IEEE 802.3) CSMA/CA (IEEE 802.11)
Physical Medium Wired (Coaxial cable, Twisted pair) Wireless (Radio frequency waves)
Collision Philosophy Detects collisions during transmission. Avoids collisions before transmission.
Action on Collision Aborts immediately; transmits Jam signal. Cannot detect in flight; discovers loss by missing ACK.
Explicit ACKs? ❌ No (Wired channel has low error rate). ✅ Yes (Mandatory ACK for every frame).
Minimum Frame Size Strictly enforced (64 bytes in Ethernet). No minimum frame size restriction.
Handshake Overhead Zero handshake; transmits immediately. High (DIFS, backoff slots, RTS/CTS, SIFS, ACK).
Section 10.12

Controlled Access Protocols (Collision-Free)

🔬 Supplementary Supplementary deep-dive / GATE detail — not directly in standard university syllabus

In Controlled Access, stations consult one another to determine who has permission to transmit. Collisions are 100% eliminated!

  • Reservation: Time is divided into intervals. Stations make reservations during a contention-free reservation mini-slot before sending data.
  • Polling: A designated Primary Station polls secondary stations in turn. Poll: "Do you have data to send?" Select: "I have data for you." (Downside: single point of failure at primary controller).
  • Token Passing (IEEE 802.5 Token Ring / 802.4 Token Bus): A special control frame called the Token circulates around the logical ring. Only the station currently holding the token has permission to transmit data frames! Provides guaranteed, deterministic latency.
Section 10.13

Channelization: CDMA & Orthogonal Chip Codes

🔬 Supplementary Supplementary deep-dive / GATE detail — not directly in standard university syllabus

Code Division Multiple Access (CDMA) allows all stations to transmit simultaneously across the exact same frequency band at the exact same time without collision!

Orthogonal Code Theory

Each station is assigned a unique $m$-bit code sequence called a Chip Sequence ($C$). The codes are mathematically orthogonal:
• Inner product of any code with itself = 1: $C_i \cdot C_i = 1$
• Inner product of any code with a different code = 0: $C_i \cdot C_j = 0\ (i \ne j)$

📝 Worked Numerical 10.1 (CDMA Transmission & Decoding)

Assign 4-bit chip codes (using $+1$ and $-1$):
• Station A: $C_A = (-1, -1, +1, +1)$
• Station B: $C_B = (-1, +1, -1, +1)$

Notice $C_A \cdot C_B = (+1) + (-1) + (-1) + (+1) = 0$ (Orthogonal!).
If Station A sends bit 1 ($+C_A$) and Station B sends bit 0 ($-C_B$):
Transmitted Composite Signal $S = C_A - C_B = (-1, -1, +1, +1) - (-1, +1, -1, +1) = \mathbf{(0, -2, +2, 0)}$.

Receiver recovers Station A's data:
Compute inner product of $S$ with $C_A$ divided by 4:
$\text{Result} = \frac{0(-1) + (-2)(-1) + 2(1) + 0(1)}{4} = \frac{0 + 2 + 2 + 0}{4} = \frac{4}{4} = \mathbf{+1\ (Bit\ 1)}$!
Station B's transmission was completely cancelled out by the dot product!

Section 10.14

Ethernet Evolution & Standards (IEEE 802.3)

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Ethernet Standards: 10Base-T, Fast Ethernet, Gigabit Ethernet)

Invented in 1973 by Robert Metcalfe at Xerox PARC, Ethernet evolved from a 2.94 Mbps experimental bus to become the universal LAN standard of the planet.

Generation Speed Standard Cable & Spec Signaling & Topology
Standard Ethernet 10 Mbps 10Base-5 (Thick coax), 10Base-2 (Thin coax), 10Base-T (Cat 3 UTP) Manchester line code, half-duplex CSMA/CD bus.
Fast Ethernet 100 Mbps 100Base-TX (Cat 5 UTP), 100Base-FX (Fiber) 4B/5B + MLT-3 line code, switched star topology.
Gigabit Ethernet 1000 Mbps (1 Gbps) 1000Base-T (Cat 5e UTP, 4 pairs simultaneous), 1000Base-SX/LX (Fiber) PAM-5 line coding, full-duplex switches (CSMA/CD obsolete).
10G / 100G Ethernet 10 Gbps to 100 Gbps 10GBase-T (Cat 6a), Optical Fiber (single/multi-mode) Full-duplex dedicated point-to-point links only.

Physical (MAC) Addressing Deep Dive

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Physical MAC 48-bit Addressing & OUI Architecture)
Figure 10.4: 48-bit MAC Address Architecture
   |================ 24 Bits ================|================ 24 Bits ================|
   | OUI (Organizationally Unique Identifier) | Device NIC Serial Number (Vendor Assigned) |
   |=========================================|=========================================|
     ^                                         Example: 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E
     |-> Bit 0 (I/G Bit): 0 = Unicast, 1 = Multicast/Broadcast
     |-> Bit 1 (U/L Bit): 0 = Globally Unique (IEEE), 1 = Locally Administered
  • Unicast Address: First byte ends in an even hex digit (least significant bit is 0). Sent to one specific device.
  • Multicast Address: First byte ends in an odd hex digit (e.g., 01:00:5E:...). Heard by an interested group of devices.
  • Broadcast Address: All 48 bits set to 1: FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF. Delivered to every host on the broadcast domain.
Section 10.16

IEEE 802.3 Ethernet Frame Structure

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (IEEE 802.3 Ethernet Frame Structure & Fields)
Figure 10.5: Standard Ethernet Frame Fields (Bytes)
   +----------+-------+----------+----------+------------+-----------------+---------+
   | Preamble |  SFD  | Dest MAC | Src MAC  | Type/Length| Data (Payload)  |   FCS   |
   |  7 Bytes | 1 Byte|  6 Bytes |  6 Bytes |   2 Bytes  | 46 - 1500 Bytes | 4 Bytes |
   +----------+-------+----------+----------+------------+-----------------+---------+
   |<--- Physical Layer --->|<----------------- Data Link Layer Frame ---------------->|
                            |<--- Minimum Frame: 64 Bytes --->|
                            |<--------------- Maximum Frame: 1518 Bytes -------------->|
Field Size Function & Specifications
Preamble 7 Bytes Alternating pattern 10101010... for receiver clock synchronization.
SFD (Start Frame Delimiter) 1 Byte Exact pattern 10101011; the ending pair of consecutive $1\text{s}$ alerts the receiver that data starts immediately next.
Destination MAC 6 Bytes 48-bit hardware address of recipient.
Source MAC 6 Bytes 48-bit hardware address of sender.
Type / Length 2 Bytes If $\ge 1536$ ($0x0600$): EtherType (e.g., $0x0800$ for IPv4, $0x0806$ for ARP). If $\le 1500$: Length field.
Data Payload (MTU) 46 to 1500 Bytes Carries the Layer 3 IP packet. Minimum 46 bytes required to guarantee 64-byte frame length; padded with zeros if smaller!
FCS (Frame Check Sequence) 4 Bytes 32-bit CRC checksum.
Section 10.17

Chapter Summary & High-Yield Exam Traps

🎓 Uni Required Required for syllabus topic: UNIT II Data link layer: MAC Protocols (Chapter Revision & Exam Traps)
⚡ High-Yield Rapid Review Points
  • Pure ALOHA: Max throughput = $18.4\%$ ($1/(2e)$) at $G = 0.5$. Vulnerable period = $2T_{\text{fr}}$.
  • Slotted ALOHA: Max throughput = $36.8\%$ ($1/e$) at $G = 1.0$. Vulnerable period = $1T_{\text{fr}}$.
  • CSMA/CD: Wired Ethernet. Minimum frame size $L_{\min} = 2 \times T_p \times B$. Slot time = 64 bytes. Binary backoff $[0, 2^i - 1]$.
  • CSMA/CA: Wireless Wi-Fi. Uses IFS, Contention window, RTS/CTS, and NAV. Solves hidden terminals.
  • CDMA: Uses orthogonal chip codes. $C_i \cdot C_j = 0$ for $i \ne j$.
  • Ethernet Frame: Min size = 64 bytes; Max size = 1518 bytes. Payload = 46 to 1500 bytes (MTU). FCS = 4 bytes CRC-32.
